# Detective - Quick Reference Guide

## Welcome, Detective! 🔍

You've been assigned to investigate cases in Threadville. Use forensic evidence, deduction, and detective work to solve crimes that have gone unsolved.

---

## Getting Started

### 1. Select Your Case
Choose from available case files at the Police Department archive. Each case presents a unique mystery.

### 2. Review the Evidence
The Investigation Board displays all available evidence:
- **Photos**: Suspect profiles, crime scenes
- **Documents**: Reports, records, statements
- **Audio**: Voice recordings, confessions
- **Biological**: Lab results, DNA analysis
- **Trace**: Physical evidence, samples
- **Digital**: Messages, emails, digital records

### 3. Examine Items Closely
Click any evidence to zoom in and read details carefully. Pay attention to:
- Names and identities
- Dates and times
- Locations
- Relationships between people
- Contradictions and inconsistencies

---

## Investigation Techniques

### Pinning Evidence 📌
Mark important evidence by clicking the pin icon. Pinned items:
- Stay highlighted on your board
- Help you organize your findings
- Let you focus on key clues

**Strategy**: Pin evidence that points to suspects or proves motive/means/opportunity.

### Linking Evidence 🔗
Create connections between pieces of evidence by clicking the link icon:
1. Click the link tool
2. Click on first piece of evidence
3. Click on related evidence to connect them

**What Links Show**:
- A suspect appearing in multiple photos
- The same person's name in documents
- Evidence from the same location
- Items that occurred on the same date
- Relationships between people

### Building Your Theory
As you create links, a pattern should emerge:
- Who had **motive** (reason to commit the crime)
- Who had **means** (ability to do it)
- Who had **opportunity** (access and timing)

---

## Evidence Analysis Tips

### Names and Identities
- Track who appears in multiple pieces of evidence
- Note aliases or different versions of names
- Identify relationships (suspect, witness, victim)

### Timeline
- Note dates and times from evidence
- Look for alibi contradictions
- Identify when suspects had opportunity

### Locations
- Track where events occurred
- Note if suspects had access to locations
- Identify crime scene connections

### Statements
- Compare what different people said
- Look for contradictions and lies
- Note who corroborates whom

### Physical Evidence
- Lab results that prove presence
- DNA or fingerprint matches
- Items linking suspects to crime

## Deduction Walkthrough

### Example: The Missing Necklace
1. **Evidence**: 
   - Police report of theft
   - Suspect profile of Jane
   - Receipt for gold necklace

2. **Links to Create**:
   - Jane's photo → Receipt (shows she bought jewelry)
   - Jane's photo → Police report (mentions her name)
   - Receipt → Police report (same date/location)

3. **Analysis**:
   - Motive: Jane owned valuables and had financial problems
   - Means: She had access to the location
   - Opportunity: She was present on the crime date

4. **Conclusion**: Links prove Jane is connected to the evidence chain

## Winning the Case

To solve a case successfully:

1. ✅ Pin all suspicious evidence
2. ✅ Create connections between related items
3. ✅ Ensure your links follow logical rules
4. ✅ Verify all connections matter to the solution
5. ✅ Identify the culprit beyond reasonable doubt

The game will tell you when you've solved the case!

---

## Common Challenges

### "I'm Stuck"
- Re-read all documents carefully
- Look for names, dates, and locations you might have missed
- Compare suspect profiles against evidence
- Try linking different items to find patterns

### "What Links Should I Make?"
- Start with clear connections (same person, same date/place)
- Then find evidence chains that tell a story
- Each link should make logical sense

### "How Do I Know I'm Right?"
- Your connections must satisfy specific logical rules
- The evidence must form a complete chain
- Cross-verify with suspect profiles and statements
- When solved, the game confirms your victory!

## Forensic Terminology

**Alibi**: Defense claim explaining where someone was during the crime

**Suspect**: Person believed to have committed the crime

**Motive**: Reason or cause for committing a crime

**Means**: Method or tool used to commit the crime

**Opportunity**: Chance and ability to commit the crime

**Evidence**: Items or information proving something in the investigation

**Chain of Custody**: Documented handling of evidence

**Lab Analysis**: Scientific examination of physical evidence
